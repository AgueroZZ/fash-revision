Question

The reviewer asked whether the dynamic eQTLs that FASH discovers show enrichment in functionally relevant enhancers. This page is a short, orthogonal check on that question: it asks whether the discovered variants fall inside enhancer regions of the cell types involved in the differentiation more often than the variants tested in this study do. The estimates below are descriptive and exploratory. They are not a fully adjusted functional-enrichment analysis, and they are not used anywhere as evidence that individual discoveries are correct.

Two sections, each comparing all discovered variants against one lead variant per gene:

  • Section 1 compares current FASH with Strober’s linear and quadratic/nonlinear analyses.
  • Section 2 compares all FASH discoveries with the subset at variants that neither Strober analysis identified.

Enhancer annotations

We used enhancer annotations from the Roadmap Epigenomics Project 15-state ChromHMM segmentation (Roadmap Epigenomics Consortium et al. 2015). We treated the Roadmap enhancer states (EnhG and Enh) as enhancer regions and focused on three epigenomes spanning the differentiation trajectory of this iPSC-to-cardiomyocyte study:

Epigenome Tissue Stage
E020 iPS-20b starting state
E013 hESC-derived CD56+ mesoderm intermediate state
E095 left ventricle terminal state

Each tested variant is treated as a single base and intersected against the enhancer intervals of each epigenome, giving one indicator per variant per epigenome over all 745,867 tested variants.

With one lead variant per gene, FASH’s 1,169 lead variants are enriched at the intermediate and terminal states: 1.47-fold (95% CI 1.15-1.88) at the E013 mesoderm enhancers and 1.26-fold (95% CI 1.04-1.53) at the E095 left-ventricle enhancers. The starting state gives no signal, 1.05-fold (95% CI 0.77-1.43). Both Strober analyses point the same way at E013 and E095, with intervals that overlap FASH’s.

Using every discovered variant the FASH estimates are smaller: 1.22-fold (95% CI 1.03-1.44) at E095, and 1.11-fold (95% CI 0.91-1.36) at E013, which is not distinguishable from the background. The Strober sets carry somewhat larger estimates in this view. The three discovery sets differ in size and membership and each has its own gene composition, so these differences are not a formal comparison between methods.

Section 2: FASH-specific discoveries

An rsID is counted as FASH-only if it appears in neither Strober analysis, regardless of which gene it was paired with; this is the conservative reading, since an enhancer annotation is a property of a variant rather than of a gene-variant pair. For the lead column, FASH’s lowest-lfdr variant is chosen for each gene first and the exclusion is applied afterwards, so a gene whose best variant Strober also found drops out rather than falling back to a weaker one. That leaves 7,981 of FASH’s 9,148 discovered variants, and 1,029 of its 1,169 lead variants.

The lead-per-gene enrichment survives the exclusion largely intact: 1.38-fold (95% CI 1.12-1.71) at E013 and 1.26-fold (95% CI 1.04-1.53) at E095, against 1.47-fold and 1.26-fold for the unfiltered FASH lead set. Taken together, FASH discoveries — including lead variants that neither Strober analysis identified — show enrichment in enhancer annotations relevant to this differentiation, which offers orthogonal support for their regulatory relevance. The signal is clearest for the mesoderm and left-ventricle states and under lead-per-gene selection.

Methods

Background. All 745,867 variants tested in this study that lie on an autosome and participate in at least one tested gene-variant pair. No external reference panel enters, so the background is exactly the cis-eQTL testing universe from which the discoveries were drawn.

Fold enrichment. For a variant set and an enhancer annotation,

\[\text{fold enrichment} = \frac{\text{proportion of selected variants overlapping the enhancer}}{\text{proportion of all tested variants overlapping the enhancer}}\]

The background excludes the set being tested, so a set is never part of its own comparator. No matched controls are used: the purpose here is a simple exploratory check, and every background variant already comes from the same testing universe.

Discovery sets. FASH uses cumulative lfdr at FDR 0.05 after the BF correction; both Strober analyses use their reported eFDR 0.05, with non_linear_dynamic_eqtls_5_pc.txt labelled quadratic/nonlinear throughout. “One lead variant per gene” keeps, for each discovered gene, the variant with the lowest lfdr (FASH) or the smallest p-value (Strober). This reduces the repeated contribution of multiple variants tagging the same signal; the all-variant view reports the complete discovery set.

Uncertainty. Uncertainty was assessed using a leave-one-autosome-out jackknife to account approximately for local dependence among variants. All intervals shown are 95% jackknife intervals.

Reproducibility

The figures on this page are computed at render time by enrichment_api.R, through exactly the calls below:

source(
  "code/revision_simulations/internal/fash_strober_enhancer_comparison/enrichment_api.R"
)

# 1. Background: every variant tested in this study.
background <- load_background()

# 2. Variant sets: the FASH and Strober discovery sets, all and lead-per-gene.
sets <- load_variant_sets()

# 3. The three Roadmap enhancer annotations.
annotations <- load_annotation_groups("custom", groups = c(
  "Roadmap E020 iPS-20b: Enhancer",
  "Roadmap E013 hESC-derived CD56+ mesoderm: Enhancer",
  "Roadmap E095 left ventricle: Enhancer"
))

# 4. Fold enrichment against the tested-variant background, with
# 5. leave-one-autosome-out jackknife 95% intervals.
compute_enrichment(sets, background, annotations, controls = "background")

compute_enrichment() takes any named list of variant vectors, so substituting your own sets needs no other change:

compute_enrichment(
  list(my_hits = readLines("my_variants.txt"), fash_only = sets$current_only_lead),
  background, annotations, controls = "background"
)
