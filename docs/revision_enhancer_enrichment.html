Question

Do the dynamic eQTLs that FASH discovers fall in functionally relevant enhancers? As an orthogonal biological check, we ask whether one lead variant per discovered gene overlaps established Roadmap enhancer annotations more often than the variants tested in this study do. We consider Roadmap epigenomes relevant to pluripotent, germ-layer, cardiac, and related vascular states, and use the enhancer definition adopted by Strober et al. (2019). The estimates below are descriptive and exploratory: they are not a fully adjusted functional enrichment analysis, and they are not evidence that any individual discovery is correct.

Two sections, both using one lead variant per discovered gene:

  • Section 1 compares current FASH with Strober’s linear and quadratic/nonlinear analyses.
  • Section 2 compares all FASH lead variants with the subset at rsIDs that neither Strober analysis identified.

Roadmap enhancer annotations

Following Strober et al. (2019), enhancer regions were defined using the Roadmap 15-state ChromHMM enhancer annotations (Enh, EnhG, EnhBiv, and BivFlnk) (Roadmap Epigenomics Consortium et al. 2015). Using an established annotation procedure avoids introducing an enhancer definition of our own. Thirteen epigenomes are displayed, in four groups:

The iPSC and heart epigenome choices follow Strober et al. (2019); the three hESC-derived germ-layer epigenomes are included because cardiomyocytes arise through mesoderm. E065 aorta is a vascular rather than a cardiac tissue, so it is kept as a separate, related vascular annotation and is never counted among the cardiac epigenomes. Each tested variant is treated as a single base and intersected with the published Roadmap enhancer intervals, giving one indicator per variant per epigenome over all 745,867 tested variants.

FASH’s 1,169 lead variants are enriched in 6 of 13 displayed annotations, over a narrow range of 1.24-1.48-fold. The annotations whose intervals lie entirely above 1 are E021, E022, E011, E012, E013, E095 — that is, two iPSC epigenomes, all three germ layers, and left ventricle. The three remaining cardiac annotations point the same way with intervals that reach or cross 1 (E083 1.19, E105 1.28, E104 1.13).

Both Strober lead sets point the same way. Their intervals are wider, which is what their smaller size would predict — 548 and 690 lead variants against FASH’s 1,169 — and where they do clear 1 it is at annotations drawn from the same groups: E022, E013, E083, E105 for linear and E013 for quadratic/nonlinear. The three discovery sets differ in size, membership and gene composition, so this is not a formal comparison between methods.

The enrichment is spread across pluripotent, germ-layer and cardiac annotations rather than concentrated in any one of them, and within each group some members clear 1 while others do not. These lead-per-gene estimates therefore support the general statement that lead variants identified by FASH are enriched in several established Roadmap enhancer annotations relevant to this differentiation system, and they do not support any claim of developmental-stage or tissue specificity. E065 aorta, the sparsest annotation in the panel and the only related vascular one, carries the single below-1 point estimate, 0.70-fold (95% CI 0.41-1.20), and rests on the fewest overlapping variants, so it should be read as neither support nor contradiction.

Section 2: FASH-specific discoveries

An rsID is counted as FASH-only if it appears in neither Strober analysis, regardless of which gene it was paired with; this is the conservative reading, since an enhancer annotation is a property of a variant rather than of a gene-variant pair. FASH’s lowest-lfdr variant is chosen for each gene first and the exclusion is applied afterwards, so a gene whose best variant Strober also found drops out rather than falling back to a weaker one. That leaves 1,029 of FASH’s 1,169 lead variants.

The enrichment survives the exclusion essentially intact. The same 6 of 13 annotations clear 1 — E021, E022, E011, E012, E013, E095 — at 1.24-1.58-fold, against 1.24-1.48-fold for the unfiltered FASH lead set, and the point estimates barely move (E013 1.39 against 1.48; E022 1.43 against 1.42; E095 1.24 against 1.24).

So the enhancer-enrichment pattern is largely retained among FASH lead variants at rsIDs that neither Strober analysis identified. Taken with Section 1, this is orthogonal support for the regulatory relevance of these discoveries as a set, resting on externally established Roadmap and Strober annotations rather than on anything fitted here. It is not validation of any individual discovery, and it does not establish specificity to a particular differentiation stage or tissue.

Methods

Background. All 745,867 variants tested in this study that lie on an autosome and participate in at least one tested gene-variant pair. No external reference panel enters, so the background is exactly the cis-eQTL testing universe from which the discoveries were drawn.

Discovery sets and lead variants. FASH uses cumulative lfdr at FDR 0.05 after the BF correction; both Strober analyses use their reported eFDR 0.05, with non_linear_dynamic_eqtls_5_pc.txt labelled quadratic/nonlinear throughout. Every set displayed here keeps, for each discovered gene, one lead variant: the variant with the lowest lfdr (FASH) or the smallest p-value (Strober). Using one lead variant per gene reduces the repeated contribution of multiple correlated variants associated with the same gene.

Enhancer annotation. Roadmap Epigenomics 15-state ChromHMM segmentations for the thirteen epigenomes above, with enhancer regions defined by the states Enh, EnhG, EnhBiv and BivFlnk as in Strober et al. (2019). The iPSC and heart epigenome choices follow the same source.

Fold enrichment. For a variant set and an enhancer annotation,

\[\text{fold enrichment} = \frac{\text{proportion of selected variants overlapping the enhancer}}{\text{proportion of the remaining tested variants overlapping the enhancer}}\]

The background excludes the set being tested, so a set is never part of its own comparator. No matched controls are used: the purpose here is a simple exploratory check, and every background variant already comes from the same testing universe. A set-annotation pair with fewer than 10 overlapping variants is reported as not estimable rather than estimated.

Uncertainty. Uncertainty was assessed using a leave-one-autosome-out jackknife to account approximately for local dependence among variants. All intervals shown are 95% jackknife intervals; nominal p-values are available in the output CSVs but are not used on this page.

Reproducibility

The figures on this page read the validated thirteen-epigenome indicator table and estimates built by run_roadmap_enhancer_exploration.R, through the shared estimator in enrichment_api.R. The annotation intervals are not rebuilt here. Exactly the calls below reproduce every estimate shown above:

source(
  "code/revision_simulations/internal/fash_strober_enhancer_comparison/enrichment_api.R"
)
source(
  "code/revision_simulations/internal/roadmap_enhancer_exploration/roadmap_enhancer_exploration_helpers.R"
)

# 1. Background: every autosomal variant tested in this study.
background <- load_background()

# 2. Discovery sets, one lead variant per discovered gene.
sets <- load_variant_sets()[c(
  "current_lead", "linear_lead", "quadratic_lead", "current_only_lead"
)]

# 3. The thirteen Roadmap epigenomes, as the indicator table built by the
#    exploration run script.
annotations <- readRDS(file.path(
  roadmap_output_directory(), "roadmap_enhancer_annotation_matrix.rds"
))

# 4. The Strober four-state enhancer definition, one column per epigenome.
strober <- grep(
  "[Strober EnhG+Enh+EnhBiv+BivFlnk]", names(annotations),
  fixed = TRUE, value = TRUE
)

# 5. Fold enrichment against the tested-variant background, with
# 6. leave-one-autosome-out jackknife 95% intervals.
compute_enrichment(
  sets,
  background,
  annotations[, c("variant_id", "chromosome", strober)],
  controls = "background"
)

compute_enrichment() takes any named list of variant vectors, so substituting your own sets needs no other change:

compute_enrichment(
  list(my_hits = readLines("my_variants.txt"), fash_only = sets$current_only_lead),
  background,
  annotations[, c("variant_id", "chromosome", strober)],
  controls = "background"
)
